Videography quotes are all over the map. One company says $1,000, another says $6,000, and nobody explains the gap. Here is the plain version, so you know what you are paying for before you sign anything.

## The short answer

The price depends on what you need filmed, not just that it is a wedding. A full-day wedding with a multi-person crew costs a lot more than a Las Vegas elopement, because the crew is working for ten hours instead of one to four.

That is why our elopement and small wedding films start at **$500**. Coverage is shorter, the day is simpler, and you still get a cinematic film.

## What you are actually paying for

Four things drive the number.

- **Hours on site.** A one hour chapel visit costs less than coverage from getting ready to the last dance.
- **Crew and gear.** One person with one camera costs less than a team running multiple angles, microphones, and lighting.
- **The edit.** A quick clip takes a fraction of the time that a color graded film with sound design takes.
- **What you receive.** Longer films, teasers, and extras all add editing hours.

## Why the cheapest quote can cost you the most

You get one wedding day, and there is no reshoot. The lowest quotes often mean one camera, no backup gear, poor audio, and a templated edit. If a card fails or the sound drops during your vows, that moment is gone.

Before you book anyone, ask these questions:

- Is the audio recorded for the ceremony, or only music over video?
- Is there backup equipment on the day?
- Is the music licensed?
- How long until I get my film?
- Can I watch a full film you made, not just a highlight reel?
