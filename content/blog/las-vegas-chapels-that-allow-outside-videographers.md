Yes, you can often bring your own videographer to a Las Vegas chapel. But not every chapel says yes, and the rules change from one to the next. Here is how to find out before you pay a deposit, and what to ask so the day goes smoothly.

## Why this question matters

Most Las Vegas chapels sell a package that includes photos, and some include video. Their in-house team is usually fine. But if you want a film made by someone who shoots cinematic work, you will want to bring your own.

A chapel that does not allow outside video is not a bad chapel. You just need to know before you book it.

## What chapels usually say

Policies fall into three groups. Always confirm the current rule with the chapel directly, because policies change.

- **Outside videographers welcome.** No extra charge.
- **Outside videographers allowed for a fee.** You pay a vendor or access fee.
- **In-house team only.** Outside photographers and videographers are not allowed.

Some chapels also set their own rules about where an outside vendor can stand, how long they can stay, and whether they can be in the room during other couples' ceremonies.

## What to ask the chapel

Send these questions by email, so you have the answers in writing.

1. Do you allow an outside videographer for our ceremony?
2. Is there a fee? When is it due?
3. How long is our ceremony slot, and is there time before or after for photos?
4. Where can the videographer stand or move during the ceremony?
5. Can we get clean audio from the officiant's microphone, or does our videographer need to place their own?
6. Are there any rooms or areas that are off limits for filming?

Audio is the one people forget. Vows are the heart of the film, and a chapel can be hard to record in. A little planning with the chapel makes a big difference.

## What to send the chapel

Short and polite works best. Something like this:

> Hi, we are getting married at your chapel on [date] at [time]. We would like to bring our own videographer, [name]. Can you confirm that is allowed, if there is a fee, and where they can stand during the ceremony? Thank you.

Keep the reply. If something changes on the day, you have it in writing.

## What your videographer needs from you

- The chapel name, date, and ceremony time
- The chapel's written OK for outside video
- The ceremony slot length and any rules about where to stand
- Where you want to go after the ceremony, for the rest of the film
